# ICAI Rules for a Chartered Accountant's Website — Detailed Study

**Position as on 28 July 2026.** Primary source: **ICAI Code of Ethics, 13th Edition (2026), Volume I**, in force **w.e.f. 1 April 2026** (announced by the Ethical Standards Board, 30 May 2026). Approved at the **447th Council Meeting, 12 December 2025**.

> ⚠️ **Most material on the internet about "ICAI website guidelines" is stale.** It describes the pre-April-2026 position (12th edition / Council General Guidelines 2008). Several rules that blogs still repeat — pull-only websites, total ban on client names, 30-day intimation of the website address to ICAI, "date of last update" on the site, search-engine keyword restrictions, 14-point font cap — **no longer appear in the current Code.** See §7.

---

## 1. Where the rules come from — the legal architecture

There is no standalone "ICAI website policy". A CA firm's website is regulated by a stack:

| Layer | Instrument | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | **Chartered Accountants Act, 1949**, First Schedule, Part I | Clause (6) and Clause (7) — breach = *professional misconduct* |
| Council guidelines | **Council Guidelines for Advertisement, 2008** No. 1-CA(7)/Council Guidelines/01/2008, *updated to December 2025*, now Chapter 3 of the Code | The operative website + write-up rules |
| Council guidelines | **Guidelines on Ethical Issues, 2026** (s. 15(2)(fa)) — Chapter 4 | Replaced the Council General Guidelines, 2008 w.e.f. 1 April 2026 |
| Code | **Code of Ethics, 13th Edition, Vol. I** (domestic) | Consolidates all of the above with Council decisions and ESB rulings |

**Clause (6), Part I, First Schedule** — a CA in practice is guilty of professional misconduct if he:

> "solicits clients or professional work either directly or indirectly by circular, advertisement, personal communication or interview or by any other means"

*Provisos:* he may request work from another CA in practice, and may respond to tenders/enquiries.

**Clause (7), Part I, First Schedule** — guilty if he:

> "advertises his professional attainments or services, or uses any designation or expressions other than chartered accountant on professional documents, visiting cards, letter heads or sign boards…"
>
> *"Provided that a member in practice may advertise through a write up, setting out the services provided by him or his firm and particulars of his firm subject to such guidelines as may be issued by the Council."*

**This proviso is the entire legal basis for a CA website.** A website is a "write up" — the Code's definition of *write up* expressly includes "**websites, which include social Networking Websites (in Push as well as in Pull mode)**" [Ch. 3.1.2(iii)].

**The consequence:** your website is lawful *only to the extent it stays inside the Council's guidelines.* Anything outside them is not merely bad practice — it is misconduct under Clause (6) or (7), actionable by the Disciplinary Directorate.

---

## 2. The governing test

Two cumulative filters apply to every element on the site:

1. **The whitelist test** — is this item in the permitted list of particulars? The lists in Ch. 3.2 and Ch. 3.3.7 are *exhaustive*, not illustrative.
2. **The solicitation test** — Ch. 3.3.14: *"The details in the Website should be so designed that it does not amount to soliciting client or professional work."* Even a whitelisted item, if presented so as to solicit, is a breach.

The Code states its own philosophy plainly [2.14.1.6(iii)]: *"The satisfaction of clients would be the best advertisement… It is the quality of service which attracts and retains the clients."*

## 3. The Website Guidelines — Chapter 3.3, clause by clause

**Title:** *"Website of the CA Firms/Network Firms/Alliance Firms"* — guidelines for posting particulars on websites by CAs in practice, firms of CAs in practice, and/or Networks/Alliances of firms registered with ICAI.

### 3.3.1–3.3.2 — Freedom to have a site, no prescribed format
CAs, CA firms, and ICAI-registered Networks/Alliances **are free to create their own website.** ICAI deliberately **does not prescribe a format** — *"to provide flexibility to the Members."* No design, colour, or layout mandate exists.

### 3.3.3 — Push and Pull technology *(major change)*
> "The websites… **may run on 'Pull' as well as 'Push' technology.** For services which are **exclusively reserved for Chartered Accountants, only 'pull' model will be permitted**… the information should be provided only on the basis of specific 'pull' request."

- **Pull** = the visitor comes looking and requests the information.
- **Push** = the firm propagates information outward (email campaigns, feeds, notifications, paid distribution).
- **"Exclusively reserved" services** = audit and attestation functions reserved to CAs by statute (Companies Act audit, tax audit, statutory certifications). The Code uses the same phrase in the tender guidelines to mean exactly this.
- **Practical effect:** GST/ITR compliance support, bookkeeping, advisory, virtual CFO, management consultancy, forensic, valuation-type non-exclusive work **may now be pushed**. Statutory audit, tax audit and attestation **may not** — that content must sit behind a visitor-initiated request.

### 3.3.4 — No circulation of exclusive-service content
Information on exclusively reserved services must not be circulated "on their own or through E-mail or by any other mode or technique except on a specific 'pull' request."

### 3.3.5 — No driving traffic to the site
> "The Chartered Accountants would also **not issue any circular or any other advertisement or any other material of any kind whatsoever by virtue of which they solicit people to visit their Website.** The Chartered Accountants would, however, be permitted to **mention their Website address on their professional stationery and email.**"

This is the clause that bites hardest on digital marketing. Read with 3.3.3, the safe reading is: **you may not run campaigns whose purpose is to drive traffic to the site.** Paid search/display advertising buying visits to the site is the paradigm case of what 3.3.5 prohibits.

### 3.3.6 — Networks and Alliances
An ICAI-registered Network/Alliance may display its **Registration Number**, and must state the **model adopted** — Alliance Model, Network Model, or Lead Firm Model.

### 3.3.7 — The permitted-content whitelist
The following **may** be displayed:

| # | Permitted item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | Member / trade / firm name / Network or Alliance name | Must match ICAI records (see 3.3.21) |
| (ii) | Year of establishment | |
| (iii) | Address (Head Office **and** Branches), Tel, Fax, E-mail ID(s) | |
| (iv) | **Peer Review status** | *New* |
| (v) | **Audit Quality Maturity Model (AQMM) level** as reviewed by a peer reviewer / AQMM Reviewer | *New* |
| (vi) | Nature of services rendered | |
| (vii) | **Exclusive services — displayable only on specific "pull" request** | The key architectural constraint |
| (viii) | Partners: Name, Year of Qualification, Other Qualification(s), Tel (Off-direct / Res.), Mobile, E-mail, **Area of Experience** | Networks may give firm-wise details |
| (ix) | Employees (Professional / Others): Name, Designation, Area of Experience | |
| (x) | Job vacancies, including articleship | |
| (xi) | No. of articled assistants | |
| (xii) | Affiliation with an ICAI-registered Network | |
| (xiii) | **Name of the client and nature of assignment handled — subject to the client's permission** | *Major change.* For **exclusively reserved** services, **only the client name** may be given, with permission. **Fee charged cannot be given.** |

**Fees — the narrow exception.** Fees may be disclosed **only where a regulator (in India or abroad) requires it**, only to the extent of that requirement, and only while that requirement subsists. Where disclosed, the site must carry, immediately below the disclosure **in italics**:

> *"This disclosure is in terms of the requirement of [name of the regulator] having jurisdiction in [name of the country/area where such regulator has jurisdiction] vide [Rule/Directive etc. under which the disclosure is required by the Regulator]."*

For a domestic Indian practice with no such regulator obligation: **no fees, no rate cards, no packages, no "starting from ₹…" anywhere on the site.**

### 3.3.8 — Network constitution must be current
Changes in constitution (entry/exit of a firm) must be updated. **No information about any entity that is not part of the Alliance/Network may appear.**

### 3.3.9 — Photographs *(liberalised)*
Permitted:
- **Passport-style photographs** of persons associated with the firm/Network/Alliance, and
- **Photo gallery of professional event(s)** organised by the firm/Network/Alliance, or any professional event where lectures are delivered by the firm's partners/proprietor.

Note the qualifier throughout: *professional* events. Ch. 3.1.3(H) separately bars photographs of events in a write-up, with a carve-out only for cultural programmes/festivals posted on social networking sites without any element of advertisement or solicitation.

### 3.3.10 — Content marketing, within limits
> "The members may include **blogs, articles, professional information, bulletin boards, professional updation and other matters of larger importance or of professional interest** on the website. **Educational videos on topics of professional relevance are permissible.**"

This is the single largest legitimate content channel available to a CA firm. Note the boundary: material of *professional interest*, not promotional material about the firm.

### 3.3.11 — Chat rooms and client portals
Chat rooms permitted among ICAI members, and between the firm and its clients, **subject to confidentiality protocol**. Firms may provide a **document management facility with distinct login and password** for clients to access copies of their documents.

### 3.3.12 — Social media links
> "The Firm can provide link of its page on Social Networking site. **However, the members should not solicit people to visit or like their respective page(s)**."

So: link out — yes. "Follow us", "Like our page", follower-drive CTAs — no.

### 3.3.13 — Online advice
Members/firms may give online advice to clients **who specifically request it**, free or paid. (Note the trigger: a specific request. Unsolicited advisory push is outside this.)

### 3.3.14 — The anti-solicitation catch-all + enforcement
The site must be designed so it "does not amount to soliciting client or professional work." If **any content or technical feature** offends the Code, the schedules to the Act, or ICAI directions, ICAI may act under its **disciplinary mechanism, suo moto or on complaint.**

Note the words **"or technical feature"** — this reaches beyond copy to pop-ups, lead-capture mechanics, exit-intent modals, retargeting pixels, auto-mailers, and chat widgets that initiate conversation.

### 3.3.15 — Confidentiality
The website must ensure adequate secrecy of client matters handled through it.

### 3.3.16 — No advertising on the site
> "**No Advertisement in the nature of banner or any other nature will be permitted on the Website.**"

Both directions: no third-party ad inventory on your site, and no banner-style self-promotion.

### 3.3.17 — Dignity
The website "should be befitting the profession of Chartered Accountants and should not contain any information or material which is unbecoming of a Chartered Accountant."

### 3.3.18 — Permitted outbound links
Links may be given to: **ICAI**, its **Regional Councils and Branches**, and websites of **Government / Government departments / Regulatory authorities / other professional bodies.** (Commercial outbound links are not in the list.)

### 3.3.19 — ICAI positions held
A proprietor/partner may mention **existing or past positions held in ICAI as an elected representative** of the Central Council, Regional Council or Branches.

### 3.3.20 — Domain name rule
> "The Website address should be **as near as possible to the individual name / trade name, firm name** of the Chartered Accountant in practice or firm… But **it should not amount to soliciting clients or professional work or advertisement of professional attainments or services.** The **Ethical Standards Board (ESB)** will decide in case there is any difficulty."

So `skla.in` or `sklahoti.com` — fine. `bestcainmumbai.com`, `taxsaverexperts.in`, `gstrefundfast.com` — not fine. When in doubt, the ESB rules.

### 3.3.21 — Consistency with ICAI records
The website must mention information **"not at material variance from the information as per the ICAI's records"** — firm name, FRN, addresses, partner details must match ICAI registration.

## 4. Adjacent rules that govern a website in practice

### 4.1 Chapter 3.1 — Conditions on the write-up (the site's copy)
Every condition below applies to website copy, because a website *is* a write-up:

| | Condition [Ch. 3.1.3] |
|---|---|
| A | It shall be **honest and truthful** |
| B | **No exaggerated claims** for services, qualifications or experience of the member, firm, partners or associated persons |
| C | **No disparaging references or unsubstantiated comparisons** to the work of others |
| D | Nothing that may **bring the profession into disrepute** |
| E | **No testimonials or endorsements** concerning members or the fees charged |
| F | **No achievements/awards** (except awards by Central/State Government or regulatory bodies), **no other position held**, **no accreditations** granted by any organisation |
| G | **No monogram of any kind and no catch words** |
| H | **No photographs of events** (carve-out: cultural programmes/festivals on social networking sites, with no advertisement or solicitation) |
| I | No violation of the Act, the Regulations, the Code of Ethics 2026, or any Council Guideline |

Also: contents must be true to the best of the member's knowledge and belief; **ICAI neither approves write-ups nor takes responsibility for them** [3.1.1]; and **ICAI may issue a reasoned directive to remove or withdraw the whole write-up or any part of it** [3.1.4].

**Condition (F) is widely underestimated.** "Ranked among…", "ISO 9001 certified", "Great Place to Work", industry-body memberships, awards from magazines or chambers of commerce — **all prohibited.** Only Central/State Government or regulatory-body awards survive.

**Condition (G) — "catch words"** — kills taglines like *"Your growth partner"*, *"Excellence in compliance"*, *"Trusted since 1985"* as decorative slogans.

### 4.2 Chapter 3.2 — Permitted particulars in a write-up
Slightly wider than the website whitelist. For **firms**: firm name + "Chartered Accountants", FRN, year of establishment, professional address(es) **registered with ICAI** (HO and branches), **working hours**, phone/mobile/fax, e-mail, **website**, number of partners, proprietor/partner particulars including **passport-style photograph**, **CA India Logo**, employee details by category (CAs / other professionals / articled & audit assistants / other employees), employee names and particulars, **services provided**, **affiliation with an ICAI-registered Network**, and **client name + nature of assignment with client permission** (client name only, for exclusive services).

For **members**, additionally: age, date of becoming ACA, date of becoming FCA, date from which COP held, recognised qualifications, **languages known**, and position held as Director/MD in an ICAI-registered Management Consultancy Company.

### 4.3 Chapter 3.4 — Third-party platforms
Where non-CA websites (banks, finance companies, newspapers, portals) invite CAs to advise through them: **only consultancy and advice** may be rendered, provided the platform does **not** carry the CA's contact address and does **not** advertise the CA's professional achievements or status beyond stating that they are Chartered Accountants. **The firm name with the suffix "Chartered Accountants" is not permitted** on such platforms.

### 4.4 Chapter 3.5 — App-based aggregators *(clarified)*
> "It is **not permissible** for members and their firms to list themselves with online **Application based service provider Aggregators** for services which are **exclusively reserved** for Chartered Accountants. However, **there is no restriction on listing for non-exclusive Services.**"

Members may list on **Government/Regulator listing platforms** (e.g. **GeM portal**), and are encouraged to use ICAI's own listing portal **CA Connect (caconnect.icai.org)**.

### 4.5 Chapter 3.6 — Directories
Name, description and address may appear in an alphabetical members' directory. In a specialised directory or "Who's Who", the member should use discretion; may give directorships, reasonable personal details and outside interests — **but must not give the names of any clients.**

### 4.6 Chapter 3.7 — Press exemptions
Permitted press advertisements bearing the firm name: (a) **recruiting staff** for the member's own office; (b) advertisements **on behalf of clients** seeking staff or acquiring/disposing of business or property; (c) **sale of a business/property** by a member acting as trustee, liquidator or receiver. They must not be more prominent than usual and the "Chartered Accountant(s)" designation must not be in bolder type than the substance. When advertising for staff, avoid expressions such as **"a well-known firm"**, and no suggestion of superiority.

### 4.7 Logos and monograms
- **Firm logos/monograms are prohibited.** The Council's December 1997 decision bars *"the use of logo/monogram of any kind/form/style/design/colour etc. whatsoever on any display material or media e.g. paper stationery, documents, visiting cards, magnetic devices, **internet**, sign board"* — and prohibits printing the member/firm name in any manner tantamounting to a logo. A stylised wordmark that reads as a logo is caught.
- **The CA India Logo** (2023 version: letters "CA" in blue with an inverted tri-colour tick on white) **is permitted**, subject to the CA India Logo Guidelines (Appendix 'L' of the Code). It may be used by all members. Use in a rubber stamp is permitted.
- For **CSR sponsorship**, a member may mention their individual name with the prefix "CA" — but **firm name and CA Logo are not permitted.**

### 4.8 Other Clause (6) prohibitions that touch digital presence
| Practice | Position |
|---|---|
| **Educational videos/audios/podcasts** on the internet | Permitted, but **must not contain the website address**. May carry the designation "Chartered Accountant", physical address, phone, e-mail, and the firm name |
| **Sharing firm profile** with a prospective client | Only in response to the prospective client's **specific query** |
| **Roving enquiries** — letters, e-mails, circulars addressed to persons likely to need a CA | **Prohibited** (tantamount to advertisement) |
| **Public interviews / press** | Must not result in publicity or highlight professional attainments; only in response to a specific question, factual in nature |
| **Books, articles, presentations** | May give designation "Chartered Accountant" and firm name; **no professional attainments** of member or firm |
| **Sponsoring events** | Not permitted (except Council-approved ICAI CPE events) |
| **Box-number advertisements** | Prohibited |
| **Coaching/teaching** | No advertising of the association via hoardings, posters, banners or any other means |
| **Sign board** | Discretion and good taste; adequate visibility; **no glow signs or lights on large boards** of the kind traders use |
| **Firm anniversary** | Silver/Golden/Diamond/Platinum/Centenary celebrations may be advertised in a newspaper or newsletter |

## 5. What is actually prohibited on a CA firm website — consolidated

1. **Client testimonials, reviews, endorsements, star ratings** — Ch. 3.1.3(E), express.
2. **Fees, rate cards, packages, discounts, "free consultation" offers** — Ch. 3.3.7(xiii), save the regulator exception.
3. **Superlatives and comparisons** — "best", "leading", "top", "No. 1", "premier", "most trusted", "better than" — Ch. 3.1.3(B)(C).
4. **Awards, rankings, accreditations, ISO/GPTW badges, chamber memberships** — Ch. 3.1.3(F), unless from Central/State Government or a regulatory body.
5. **Logos, monograms, stylised firm marks, catch words/taglines** — Council decision Dec 1997 + Ch. 3.1.3(G). Only the CA India Logo is permitted.
6. **Banner advertising of any nature, on or by the site** — Ch. 3.3.16.
7. **Campaigns soliciting visits to the website** — Ch. 3.3.5. Paid search/display buying traffic to the site is the paradigm case.
8. **Soliciting follows/likes on social pages** — Ch. 3.3.12.
9. **Unsolicited circulation of exclusive-service content** by e-mail or any other technique — Ch. 3.3.4.
10. **Any particular not on the whitelist** — Ch. 3.3.7 read with 3.2 is exhaustive.
11. **Domain names that advertise or solicit** — Ch. 3.3.20.
12. **Information at material variance from ICAI records** — Ch. 3.3.21.
13. **Technical features that solicit** — pop-ups, lead-gen modals, unsolicited chat initiation — Ch. 3.3.14 ("or technical feature").

## 6. What is clearly permitted — the legitimate playbook

- **Full factual firm profile**: name, FRN, year of establishment, HO and branch addresses, working hours, phone, e-mail.
- **Partner and staff pages**: names, year of qualification, other qualifications, direct/residence/mobile numbers, e-mail, **area of experience**, passport-style photographs. This is generous — a real credibility asset.
- **Services listing**, with **exclusive (audit/attestation) services behind a pull request** and non-exclusive services freely presented.
- **Peer Review status and AQMM level** — genuine, ICAI-sanctioned quality signals; the correct substitute for the prohibited certifications and awards.
- **Client names and nature of assignment, with written client permission** (client name only for exclusive services). *New under the 13th edition.*
- **Blogs, technical articles, professional updates, bulletin boards, educational videos** on matters of professional interest — the principal permitted growth channel.
- **Photo gallery of professional events** organised by the firm or where its partners spoke.
- **Client portal** with individual login/password for document access, and **chat rooms** with confidentiality protocol.
- **Job and articleship vacancies.**
- **Links** to ICAI, Regional Councils, Branches, Government departments and regulators.
- **ICAI elected positions** held, past or present.
- **CA India Logo**, per Appendix 'L'.
- **Website address on professional stationery and e-mail** — Ch. 3.3.5, express.
- **Listing on GeM and on ICAI's CA Connect**; listing on aggregators for **non-exclusive** services only.

## 7. What changed on 1 April 2026 — and the stale rules to stop citing

| Issue | Pre-1 April 2026 | Current (13th edition) |
|---|---|---|
| Technology model | **Pull only** | **Pull and Push**; pull-only for exclusively reserved services [3.3.3] |
| Client names | **Not permitted** | **Permitted with client's consent**, along with nature of assignment; client name only for exclusive services [3.3.7(xiii)] |
| Intimating website address to ICAI within 30 days | Required | **No such requirement in the current text** |
| "Date up to which updated" on the site | Required | **No such requirement**; replaced by "not at material variance from ICAI's records" [3.3.21] |
| Search-engine listing restricted to "Chartered Accountants"/"CA"/"Indian CA" | Stipulated | **No such restriction in the current text** |
| Write-up font size | Capped at **14 points** | **Cap removed**; write-up is now in "**contemporary form**" [3.1.2(iii)] |
| Write-up format | Text-heavy | Definition now contemplates contemporary presentation, expressly including social networking websites in push and pull mode |
| Peer Review status / AQMM level | Not listed | **Expressly permitted** [3.3.7(iv),(v)] |
| Educational videos | Not addressed | **Expressly permitted** on the website [3.3.10] |
| Photo gallery | Restricted | **Professional-event gallery expressly permitted** [3.3.9] |
| Network/Alliance websites | Limited | **Expressly permitted**, with Registration Number and networking model displayed [3.3.1, 3.3.6] |
| Aggregator listings | Broadly discouraged | **Permitted for non-exclusive services**; barred for exclusive services; GeM and CA Connect encouraged [3.5] |
| Underlying general guidelines | Council General Guidelines, 2008 | **Guidelines on Ethical Issues, 2026** (replaced the 2008 guidelines w.e.f. 1 April 2026) |

**Caution on the removals.** The disappearance of the 30-day intimation, the update-date and the search-engine-keyword clauses means those *specific* obligations are gone from the Code's text. It does **not** create positive permission to do the opposite: Ch. 3.3.5 (no soliciting visits to the site), Ch. 3.3.14 (no soliciting design or technical feature) and Clause (6) itself continue to govern. Aggressive SEO or paid search aimed at capturing prospective clients remains exposed under those clauses, notwithstanding that the old keyword restriction has been dropped. Where a specific plan is borderline, **Ch. 3.3.20 expressly routes the question to the Ethical Standards Board** — a written ESB query is the safe course, and the ESB's practice is to answer.

## 8. Compliance checklist for a firm website

**Identity and structure**
- [ ] Firm name exactly as registered with ICAI; **FRN displayed**
- [ ] HO and branch addresses match ICAI records
- [ ] Domain name derived from the firm/trade name; nothing promotional
- [ ] No firm logo or monogram; no stylised mark reading as a logo. CA India Logo only, per Appendix 'L'
- [ ] No tagline or catch words

**Content**
- [ ] No fees anywhere (unless the regulator exception applies, with the italicised disclosure)
- [ ] No testimonials, reviews, ratings, endorsements
- [ ] No superlatives, rankings, comparisons, success claims
- [ ] No awards/accreditations except Central/State Government or regulatory-body awards
- [ ] Peer Review status / AQMM level used in their place, if held
- [ ] Client names and assignment details only with **documented written client consent**; client name only for audit/attestation clients; retain the consents
- [ ] Partner/staff profiles limited to the 3.3.7(viii)/(ix) fields
- [ ] Blog/articles/videos confined to professional subject matter, not firm promotion

**Architecture**
- [ ] Audit and attestation service content sits behind a **specific visitor-initiated request** (a form/enquiry gate), not on an open landing page
- [ ] Non-exclusive services may be presented and pushed freely
- [ ] No banner advertising, on-site or third-party
- [ ] No pop-ups, exit-intent modals, lead-capture overlays, or chat widgets that initiate the conversation
- [ ] Social links present but no "follow/like us" solicitation
- [ ] Outbound links limited to ICAI, Regional Councils/Branches, Government and regulators
- [ ] Client portal, if any, has distinct login/password and confidentiality controls

**Off-site**
- [ ] No advertising campaign whose purpose is to drive traffic to the site
- [ ] No unsolicited e-mail circulation of exclusive-service material
- [ ] No aggregator listings for exclusive services; non-exclusive listings acceptable
- [ ] Educational videos on the internet carry no website address
- [ ] Firm profile shared only in response to a specific prospective-client query

## 9. Enforcement

Breach is not a soft matter. Ch. 3.3.14 provides that ICAI may act **suo moto or on complaint** through its disciplinary mechanism under the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949. Ch. 3.1.4 empowers ICAI to direct **removal or withdrawal of the whole write-up or any part of it**. A finding of professional misconduct under Part I of the First Schedule is dealt with by the Board of Discipline, with outcomes ranging from **reprimand** and **fine** to **removal of the member's name from the Register**.

Note also Ch. 3.1.1: **ICAI will not pre-approve a write-up** and takes no responsibility for its contents. There is no clearance mechanism — the member carries the risk. The only forward-looking comfort available is an **ESB opinion**, which Ch. 3.3.20 contemplates for website-related difficulties.
