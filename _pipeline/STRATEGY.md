# Content Strategy — S K Lahoti Associates (skla.in)

*Prepared June 2026. A blog/insights engine to win organic search across all three SKLA
audiences: foreign investors entering India, NRIs/OCIs, and Indian businesses.*

---

## Why this strategy

The current site is six strong service pages plus supporting pages — good for converting
people who already know SKLA exists. What it has no machinery for is **getting found** by
someone who doesn't yet know the firm: an NRI Googling "how much TDS on selling my flat in
Hyderabad," a US founder searching "how to open a subsidiary in India," a startup owner
asking "do I need GST registration." Those are the highest-intent moments in a CA
relationship, and right now a competitor's blog answers them, not SKLA's.

This plan builds an **insights/blog section** that captures that demand. The mechanism is
*topical authority*: instead of one thin page per keyword, we publish a deep "pillar" guide
on each core topic and surround it with focused articles on the specific questions people
actually search — all interlinked. Google rewards the firm that demonstrably covers a topic
the most completely. For a 35-year firm with real cross-border expertise, this is a winnable
game, because most competitors write generic filler and SKLA can write from genuine mandate
experience.

Two timing advantages worth moving on now:

- **The Income Tax Rules 2026** rename core NRI forms (Form 13 → **Form 128** for lower-TDS
  certificates; 15CA/15CB → **Form 145/146**). Most ranking articles still use the old names.
  Fresh, correctly-named content can leapfrog stale pages.
- **GST changes from 1 Jan 2026** (e-invoicing threshold dropped to ₹5 cr, auto-suspension
  for missing bank details, the 3-year filing cutoff) created a wave of "what changed"
  searches that older content answers wrongly.

## Target audience

Three segments, served equally, each at a different search mindset:

**Foreign investors** (companies and founders entering India). Highest deal value, lowest
search volume, longest research cycle. They search in English, often from the US, UK, UAE,
Singapore, Germany. Pain: India looks legally opaque and they fear getting the entry
structure wrong. They want one partner who has done it before. Keywords are commercial and
specific ("FC-GPR filing process," "wholly owned subsidiary vs branch office India").

**NRIs & OCIs** (global Indians). Highest search volume of the three, strong emotional pull
(property, family money), recurring need. They search from the US, UAE, UK, Canada,
Australia. Pain: managing Indian tax and property from abroad, fear of overpaying TDS, fear
of repatriation getting stuck. Keywords are concrete and transactional ("lower TDS
certificate NRI property," "repatriate money from NRO account").

**Indian businesses** (domestic SMEs, startups, professionals). Largest local pool, most
competitive, shortest research cycle. They search from Hyderabad/Telangana and pan-India.
Pain: staying compliant without it eating their week. Keywords mix local intent ("CA firm in
Secunderabad," "GST registration Hyderabad") with informational ("tax audit limit,"
"DPIIT recognition benefits").

## Search-intent mapping

Match the format to the intent — a comparison query doesn't want a 4,000-word essay, and a
"how to" query doesn't want a sales page.

| Intent | What they want | Content type | Example from our plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | Understand a rule | Guide / explainer | "NRI residential status rules" |
| How-to | Complete a task | Step-by-step | "How to file FC-GPR" |
| Commercial | Decide between options | Comparison | "Subsidiary vs branch office" |
| Local-commercial | Hire someone nearby | Service page + local proof | "CA firm in Secunderabad" |
| Transactional | Act now | Service page CTA | services.html#nri |

Informational and how-to articles feed the top of the funnel; each one links to the service
page that closes the loop. We don't try to "sell" inside a how-to guide beyond a single, calm
call-to-action — that's what earns the trust that converts later.

## Internal linking plan

Internal links are how topical authority compounds — they tell Google these pages belong
together, and they move visitors toward the service pages that convert.

Three rules:

1. **Hub and spoke.** Every cluster article links *up* to its pillar with descriptive anchor
   text ("our full guide to NRI tax in India"), and the pillar links *down* to every cluster
   article. Sibling articles link across where relevant (the TDS-on-sale article links to the
   lower-TDS-certificate article, since they're read together).

2. **Always close to a service page.** Each article links once, naturally, to the matching
   service section — NRI articles to `services.html#nri`, FEMA articles to `services.html#fema`,
   GST/tax to `services.html#tax`, company-structure to `services.html#advisory`. This is the
   path from "I was just researching" to "I should call them."

3. **Surface the blog from the main nav and footer.** Add an "Insights" (or "Resources") link
   to the 7-item nav across all pages, and feature 2–3 latest articles on the homepage. Without
   this, the blog stays orphaned and Google crawls it less. (Per the project conventions,
   nav/footer changes must be applied to *every* page.)

Anchor-text discipline: vary the wording, describe the destination, never use "click here."

## Production notes (so this is actually writeable)

- **Write from real experience, not generic filler.** The differentiator is that SKLA has
  done these mandates. A line like "in practice the AO usually asks for…" beats any
  competitor's textbook summary and is exactly what earns links and trust.
- **Get the 2026 details right** — they're the edge. Use the new form names (128, 145, 146),
  the current GST thresholds, the post-July-2024 LTCG rate (12.5% without indexation). Stale
  numbers are the fastest way to lose credibility with a tax-literate reader.
- **One author voice, lightly bylined to a partner.** "Reviewed by CA. Sunil Kumar Lahoti"
  adds E-E-A-T (experience/expertise/authority/trust) that Google weighs heavily for finance.
- **Match the site's editorial design** — Newsreader headings, Inter body, the same template as
  existing pages. Each post needs the full SEO head + Article/FAQ JSON-LD, like the other pages.
- **AI-assist drafting, partner-review facts.** Reasonable to draft fast, but every number and
  rule must be checked by someone at the firm before publishing. This is regulated-advice
  territory; a wrong figure is worse than no article.

## Success metrics

Track monthly; expect organic results to build over 3–6 months (SEO is not instant).

**Traffic**
- Organic sessions to the blog: target 500/mo by Month 4, 1,500/mo by Month 6.
- Assisted conversions: enquiries from contact form where the visitor first landed on a blog
  article (tag the form source).

**Rankings — track these as the early signal**
- NRI: "lower tds certificate nri property," "tds on sale of property by nri," "repatriate
  money from nro account," "dtaa for nri."
- Investor: "open subsidiary in india," "fc-gpr filing," "branch office vs subsidiary india."
- Business/local: "ca firm in secunderabad," "gst registration hyderabad," "tax audit limit."

**Production KPIs**
- Articles published vs. planned (target ≥ 90% of calendar).
- Average words and internal links per article (≥ 1,500 words, ≥ 3 internal links).
- % of articles with FAQ schema and a partner review byline (target 100%).

**Leading indicators that it's working** — even before traffic moves: pages getting indexed
within a week, impressions climbing in Search Console, and the first few articles cracking
the top 20 for their target keyword. Those come first; clicks and enquiries follow.
