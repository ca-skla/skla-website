What Changed in GST from January 2026

Reviewed by CA. Shradha Lahoti Updated September 2026 4 min read

By January 2026 a cluster of GST rules introduced between 2023 and 2025 was fully in force, and several of them catch businesses out only when something breaks — a return that won’t file, a registration that has been suspended, an e-invoice that gets rejected. Here is what each rule says, the notification that brought it in, and what to do about it.

1. E-invoicing applies from ₹5 crore

The e-invoicing threshold is ₹5 crore aggregate annual turnover, lowered from ₹10 crore by Notification No. 10/2023–Central Tax of 10 May 2023, with effect from 1 August 2023. If your turnover crossed ₹5 crore in any financial year from 2017–18 onward, you must generate e-invoices through an Invoice Registration Portal. The threshold is not new, but it is the one that pulls in the largest band of mid-sized businesses, and it is easy to miss when turnover crosses the line part-way through a year.

There is a related timing rule. According to the e-invoice portal advisories, a business with aggregate turnover of ₹10 crore or more must report an invoice (or credit or debit note) to the portal within 30 days of the invoice date, failing which the portal rejects it; that window has applied since 1 April 2025. Businesses between ₹5 crore and ₹10 crore are outside the 30-day limit for now, but reporting promptly is still the safer habit.

2. Registrations can be suspended over bank details

Rule 10A of the CGST Rules requires bank account details to be furnished within 30 days of registration or before the first GSTR-1 (or invoice furnishing facility) is filed, whichever is earlier. Under rule 21A(2A), a contravention of rule 10A leads to system-based suspension of the registration, intimated in Form GST REG-31. Both provisions came in through Notification No. 38/2023–Central Tax of 4 August 2023. While suspended, the business may not make any taxable supply or issue a tax invoice (rule 21A(3)) — which means dispatches stop. This is an easy one to get caught by after a change of bank, so keep the details on the portal current and validated.

3. The three-year filing cut-off

A GST return cannot be furnished once three years have passed from its due date. The bar sits in sections 37(5), 39(11), 44(2) and 52(15) of the CGST Act, inserted by the Finance Act, 2023 and in force from 1 October 2023 (Notification No. 28/2023–Central Tax of 31 July 2023), and the GST portal now enforces it when a return is attempted. Once the window closes the return simply cannot be filed, and any input tax credit tied to it is lost for good. If you have old, unfiled periods, close them now — after the cut-off there is no remedy.

4. Tighter portal access

The e-way bill and e-invoice systems have also moved to multi-factor authentication, phased in by turnover band through portal advisories. That is not dramatic on its own, but with the rules above it rewards businesses that keep their portal logins, bank details and filings clean and current.

What to do this quarter

Confirm whether you have crossed the ₹5 crore e-invoicing line and set up e-invoicing if so. Verify your bank details on the portal. Identify and file any returns approaching the three-year cut-off before the window closes. If any of this is unclear, see taxation services for the nature of services.

The three-year cut-off is the one with no second chance. Everything else can be fixed late; a time-barred return cannot.
