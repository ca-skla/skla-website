The Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, 2026: who can use the window before 31 December 2026

Reviewed by CA. Sunil Kumar Lahoti Updated September 2026 9 min read

A foreign bank account opened during years abroad, a flat bought from overseas salary, a portfolio never carried into an Indian return: the Finance Act, 2026 has opened a one-time window for these cases. The Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, 2026 runs from 16 August to 31 December 2026, prices each category in advance, and brings Black Money Act immunity for what is declared. Here is who it covers, what it costs and how it runs.

What changed

Chapter IV of the Finance Act, 2026 (sections 130 to 144) enacts the Scheme; the CBDT notified the Rules and four forms on 14 August 2026 (Notification No. 114/2026, G.S.R. 732(E)), and the Scheme came into force on 16 August 2026. Three dates matter: the valuation date, 31 March 2026; the opening, 16 August 2026; and the last date, 31 December 2026. Everything is filed electronically. Two categories follow.

CategoryWhat is declaredAmount payableConditionFrom when
1. UndisclosedAn undisclosed asset located outside India, or undisclosed foreign incomeTax at 30% of the asset’s value on 31 March 2026 and of the income, plus an amount equal to 100% of that taxAggregate up to ₹1 crore16 August to 31 December 2026
2. Unreported, not untaxedA foreign asset acquired from foreign income while non-resident, or from income already taxed in India, but not declared in the return’s ScheduleA fee of ₹1 lakhAsset value up to ₹5 crore16 August to 31 December 2026

The Rules illustrate the first category: a foreign bank account valued at ₹60 lakh plus undisclosed foreign income of ₹20 lakh is an aggregate of ₹80 lakh; tax at 30% is ₹24 lakh, the additional amount another ₹24 lakh, and the declarant pays ₹48 lakh. In the second category the money was taxed, or earned abroad while non-resident, and only unreported, so the fee is flat.

The short version

Foreign assets or income left out of an Indian return can be declared in Form 1 until 31 December 2026, valued as on 31 March 2026. Untaxed amounts cost 30% plus an equal amount (aggregate up to ₹1 crore); assets that were taxed or earned while non-resident but not reported cost a ₹1 lakh fee (value up to ₹5 crore).

Who is affected

The CBDT’s FAQs describe the eligible declarant as a person resident in India in the relevant previous year, or a non-resident or RNOR who was resident in the year the undisclosed income relates to or the year the asset was acquired. The grounds: no return was filed; a return left the asset or income out; or it escaped assessment. Which years were resident years turns on the tests in our guide to NRI residential status and RNOR.

Two groups fit: a resident who kept a foreign account, property or portfolio out of the return, and the returning NRI who bought assets abroad, came home, and never listed them in the foreign-asset schedule — the reason the ₹1 lakh category exists. A person who was always non-resident has nothing to declare.

Two situations are outside the Scheme according to the FAQs: proceeds of crime with proceedings initiated or pending under the Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002, and income or assets of an assessment year already assessed under the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015. The value ceilings are eligibility conditions, not just prices.

What to do now

Start with valuation, because the categories are tested on it: fair market value on 31 March 2026: the higher of cost of acquisition and the open-market price that day. For immovable property a report from a valuer recognised by the government of the country concerned may be obtained; without one, the indexed cost of acquisition is treated as the fair market value.

A foreign bank account is valued differently: its value is the sum of every deposit from the day it was opened up to 31 March 2026 — not the balance. A deposit funded by a withdrawal from the same account is excluded, so money moved out and back is not counted twice. Values are reported in rupees at the RBI reference rate on 31 March 2026.

Form 1 is then filed electronically with supporting documents and any valuation report. An order in Form 2 states the amount payable within one month from the end of the month of the declaration. Payment is due within two months from the end of the month the order is received; up to two further months are allowed with simple interest at 1% per month or part of a month. Beyond four months from the end of the month of the order, the benefit is lost for that declaration. In the Rules’ illustration, an order of 22 September 2026 is payable by 30 November 2026 without interest and by 31 January 2027 at the latest.

Payment is reported in Form 3 with proof. Form 4, certifying that the declaration is valid and paid, follows within one month from the end of the month of that intimation.

A variance of up to 20% between the value declared and the value later determined does not by itself make the declaration invalid for misrepresentation or false particulars.

The valuation date does the heavy lifting. Most of the effort is not the form; it is reconstructing every deposit into a foreign account from the day it was opened — statements going back years, not a current balance.

What a declaration does — and does not — do

A declarant who makes a valid declaration and pays what is due is granted immunity from any further tax or penalty, and from prosecution, under the Black Money Act for the income or asset declared, for the previous year ending 31 March 2026 or any earlier year: section 139 of the Finance Act, 2026.

The FAQs also mark the limits: the declared amount is not included again in total income, but the declaration cannot be used to seek rectification or revision of an assessment already made, or set-off or relief in an appeal. A declaration is deemed invalid if any material particular is found false at any stage or any condition of the Scheme is violated.

What has not changed

The ordinary reporting obligation continues: a resident with foreign assets must still disclose them in every return, and the Black Money Act remains in force for anything left outside the Scheme. It is a window, not a repeal.

The Scheme speaks the language of the Income-tax Act, 1961, because every year it can cover ended on or before 31 March 2026; returns for tax year 2026-27 onward are filed under the Income-tax Act, 2025, as explained in our guide to NRI taxation under the Income-tax Act 2025. The firm’s NRI and expatriate services cover residential status and foreign-asset reporting. Whether a declaration is appropriate, and in which category, turns on each year’s facts; obtain advice on your facts before acting.

Frequently asked questions

According to the CBDT’s FAQs, a person resident in India in the relevant previous year, or a non-resident or RNOR who was resident in the year the income arose or the asset was acquired, who did not file a return, left the asset or income out, or whose asset or income escaped assessment. The category’s value limit must also be met.

For an undisclosed foreign asset or income, tax at 30% of the asset’s value on 31 March 2026 and of the income, plus an equal amount, where the aggregate is up to ₹1 crore. For a foreign asset bought from income earned while non-resident or already taxed in India, and left out of the return, a fee of ₹1 lakh where the asset is worth up to ₹5 crore.

Not at its balance. The value is the sum of all deposits made into the account from the day it was opened up to 31 March 2026, converted into rupees at the RBI reference rate on that date. A deposit that came out of a withdrawal from the same account is excluded.
